The Neuroscience

Your brain runs on
nutrients, not willpower.

Every neurotransmitter is synthesized from dietary precursors. Understanding these four pathways rewires how you think about food — forever.

Neurotransmitter 01
Serotonin

Your "wellbeing" molecule. 90% is produced in the gut, not the brain — through the enteric nervous system. Low serotonin drives sadness, anxiety, and compulsive eating. Its dietary precursor is tryptophan, which crosses the blood-brain barrier with the help of complex carbohydrates.

Turkey Eggs Oats Pumpkin Seeds Fermented Kefir Dark Chocolate
Neurotransmitter 02
Dopamine

Your "motivation and reward" molecule. Drives focus, pleasure, ambition, and follow-through. Built from the amino acid tyrosine. When depleted: brain fog, procrastination, and emotional eating for rapid dopamine "hits." Chronically low dopamine is linked to ADHD and depression.

Avocado Almonds Beets Bananas Green Tea (L-theanine) Wild Salmon
Stress Hormone
Cortisol

The primary stress hormone produced by your adrenal glands. Chronically elevated cortisol destroys gut microbiome diversity, depletes magnesium reserves, raises blood sugar, and drives intense cravings for high-fat, high-sugar comfort foods — creating a self-reinforcing stress-eating cycle.

Ashwagandha Dark Leafy Greens Blueberries Chamomile Tea Magnesium-rich Seeds Omega-3 Fish
Inhibitory Neurotransmitter
GABA

Your brain's primary "calm and inhibit" signal. GABA quiets overactive neurons, reduces anxiety, and allows the nervous system to downregulate after stress. Low GABA produces racing thoughts, inability to relax, and chronic tension. Magnesium, fermented foods, and specific amino acids directly support GABA production.

Fermented Vegetables Spinach Lentils Sweet Potato Walnuts Kefir
